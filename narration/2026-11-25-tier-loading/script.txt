Pro movers don't just stack things in a truck. They build it in walls, called tiers, from the cab all the way to the door. Here's how it works.

Each tier fills the truck floor to ceiling and wall to wall. Then you strap it before starting the next one, so nothing has room to slide.

Start at the cab end with your heaviest, sturdiest pieces: the fridge, the washer, dressers. Weight up front and low keeps the truck stable on the road. Balance the left side against the right.

Mattresses and box springs go in bagged and standing up along the side walls. They protect everything else like a cushion.

Build each tier in layers: heavy on the bottom, medium in the middle, light on top. Sturdy dressers make a great base. Pad the top first, then stack boxes on it.

Couches can stand on end if their frame allows it, fully wrapped in blankets. Protect the arms, and never lean heavy pieces against glass, mirrors or a TV screen.

Fill the gaps with pillows, cushions and soft bags. Snug, but not crushed.

Strap each finished tier to the truck's anchor points before you build the next one. That's what keeps everything still through braking and turns.

Wrap every piece of furniture, so no wood ever touches wood. The rule pros live by: every item protected, supported and secured.
